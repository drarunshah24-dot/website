Premature ejaculation (PE) may occur from the beginning of sexual activity (lifelong PE) or develop later after a period of normal sexual function (acquired PE). It is not a sign of weakness or lack of masculinity.

**Common Features**

- Ejaculation that occurs sooner than he would like.
- Difficulty delaying ejaculation.
- A feeling of little control over ejaculation.
- Frustration, embarrassment, or anxiety during sexual activity.
- Reduced sexual satisfaction for himself or his partner.
- Avoidance of sexual intimacy because of worry about ejaculation.
- Occasional early ejaculation is common and does not necessarily mean that you have PE.

**What Causes Premature Ejaculation?**

- Psychological Factors
- Performance anxiety
- Stress or emotional problems
- Relationship difficulties
- Depression or anxiety
- Previous negative sexual experiences
- Physical or Medical Factors
- Erectile dysfunction
- Prostate or urinary problems
- Thyroid disorders
- Hormonal or neurological factors
- Certain medications or recreational substances
- Increased sensitivity in some individuals

**Frequently Asked Questions**
*Is premature ejaculation common?*
Yes. It is one of the most common male sexual problems.

*Is premature ejaculation curable?*

PE is highly treatable. Treatment can improve ejaculation control and sexual satisfaction, although the most appropriate approach differs between individuals.

*Does PE mean that I am impotent?*

No. Premature ejaculation and erectile dysfunction are different conditions. However, they can occur together.

*Is PE caused by masturbation?*

No. Masturbation itself does not cause permanent premature ejaculation. However, habitual patterns of very rapid stimulation may sometimes influence sexual habits.

*Can stress cause premature ejaculation?*

Yes. Anxiety, stress, and performance pressure can contribute to PE or make it worse.

*Will condoms help?*

They may help some men by reducing penile sensitivity.

*Can my partner help with treatment?*

Yes. Open communication and involvement of the partner can be helpful, particularly when behavioral techniques or sex therapy are used.

*Should I see a doctor?*

You should consider seeing a urologist, andrologist, or sexual-health specialist if ejaculation problems are persistent, distressing, or affecting your relationship.
