  What is a ureteric stone?


  A ureteric stone is a hard deposit made of minerals and salts that forms in
  the kidney and moves into the ureter (the tube that carries urine from the
  kidney to the bladder). As the stone travels, it may block urine flow and
  cause severe pain.


  Common Symptoms


  You may experience:


  -Sudden, severe pain in the side (flank), back, or lower abdomen

  -Pain spreading to the groin or genital area

  -Blood in the urine (pink, red, or brown urine)

  -Burning or pain while passing urine

  -Nausea and vomiting


  Seek Emergency Medical Care Immediately If You Have:

  -Fever (≥38°C) or chills

  -Severe pain not relieved by medicines

  -Unable to pass urine

  -Persistent vomiting and inability to drink fluids

  These symptoms may indicate an infected or completely blocked kidney, which
  requires urgent treatment.


  How is it Diagnosed?


  Treatment

  1. Small Stones (Usually < 5–6 mm)

  Most small stones pass naturally.

  Treatment includes:

  -Pain-relieving medicines

  -Medicines that help the stone pass (such as tamsulosin, if appropriate)

  -Drinking enough fluids to stay hydrated

  -Straining urine to collect the stone if advised


  2. Larger Stones or Stones That Do Not Pass (Usually > 7 mm)

  If the stone is large, causes persistent pain, blocks the kidney, or is
  associated with infection, a procedure may be needed:

  Ureteroscopy (URS): A small telescope is passed through the urinary passage to
  remove or break the stone with a laser.

  Flexible URS (RIRS): Usually used for upper ureteric calculi or ureteric stone
  with kidney stone


  At Home

  -Drink enough water unless your doctor advises otherwise.

  -Take medicines exactly as prescribed.

  -Stay physically active if comfortable.

  -Watch for fever or worsening pain.

  -Attend your follow-up appointment and imaging tests.


  Preventing Future Stones

  -Drink enough water to produce at least 2–2.5 litres of urine daily.

  -Reduce salt intake.

  -Eat a balanced diet with normal calcium intake (do not stop calcium unless
  advised).

  -Limit excessive animal protein.

  -Reduce sugary soft drinks.

  -Eat more fruits and vegetables.

  -Maintain a healthy body weight.


  If you have recurrent stones, your doctor may recommend a metabolic evaluation
  and stone analysis.


  Frequently Asked Questions


  Will the stone pass on its own?

  Many stones smaller than 5 mm pass naturally within a few weeks. Larger stones
  are less likely to pass without treatment.


  Can stones come back?

  Yes. Without preventive measures, many people develop another stone within
  several years.


  Can I work during treatment?

  Most people can continue light activities as tolerated, but severe pain may
  require rest.


  Do I need surgery?

  Many stones pass naturally. Surgery or other procedures are recommended only
  when necessary.


  Follow-Up

  Please return for follow-up as advised. Even if your pain improves, a stone
  may still be present and require confirmation with imaging.
