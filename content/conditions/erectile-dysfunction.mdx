Occasional difficulty getting an erection is common and may occur because of stress, tiredness, anxiety, or alcohol. However, repeated or persistent problems should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

**Common Symptoms**

- Difficulty getting an erection
- Difficulty maintaining an erection
- An erection that is not firm enough for intercourse
- Erections that disappear during sexual activity
- Reduced confidence during sexual activity
- Anxiety about sexual performance
- Reduced sexual satisfaction

ED may occur together with premature ejaculation, reduced sexual desire, or other sexual problems.

**Causes of Erectile Dysfunction**

ED may have physical, psychological, or mixed causes.

*Physical Causes*

- Diabetes
- High blood pressure
- High cholesterol
- Heart and blood-vessel disease
- Obesity
- Smoking
- Kidney disease
- Low testosterone or other hormonal disorders
- Neurological disorders
- Pelvic or spinal injury
- Prostate or pelvic surgery
- Certain medications

*Psychological Causes*

- Stress
- Performance anxiety
- Depression
- Relationship difficulties
- Emotional problems
- Previous negative sexual experiences
- Lack of sleep or severe fatigue

**Treatment of Erectile Dysfunction**

Treatment depends on the underlying cause and your individual health.

*1. Lifestyle Changes*

Healthy lifestyle habits can improve erectile and cardiovascular health.

- Exercise regularly.
- Maintain a healthy body weight.
- Stop smoking.
- Limit excessive alcohol consumption.
- Avoid recreational drugs.
- Eat a balanced diet.
- Control diabetes, blood pressure, and cholesterol.
- Get adequate sleep.
- Reduce stress.

**Psychological and Relationship Support**
When anxiety, stress, depression, or relationship difficulties contribute to ED, counseling or sex therapy may be helpful.

- Individual counseling
- Sex therapy
- Cognitive behavioral therapy
- Couple-based therapy
- Treatment of anxiety or depression when appropriate


**Frequently Asked Questions**

*Is erectile dysfunction common?*

Yes. ED is common, particularly with increasing age, but it is not an inevitable part of aging.

*Does ED mean I am infertile?*

No. Erectile dysfunction and infertility are different conditions. A man with ED may still produce healthy sperm.

*Is ED caused only by psychological problems?*

No. ED can be caused by physical factors, psychological factors, or a combination of both.

*Can diabetes cause ED?*

Yes. Diabetes can damage blood vessels and nerves involved in erections and is an important risk factor for ED.

*Can ED be cured?*

Many men experience significant improvement with appropriate treatment. Treating underlying medical conditions and improving lifestyle can also improve erectile function.

*Are ED medicines safe?*

They are safe for many men when appropriately prescribed, but they are not suitable for everyone. Men taking nitrate medicines must not use PDE-5 inhibitors.

*Can I take sildenafil without consulting a doctor?*

It is best to discuss your symptoms and medical history with a healthcare professional before using sildenafil, particularly if you have heart disease or take other medicines.

*Can my partner be involved in treatment?*

Yes. Open communication and partner involvement can improve treatment outcomes and reduce performance anxiety.
